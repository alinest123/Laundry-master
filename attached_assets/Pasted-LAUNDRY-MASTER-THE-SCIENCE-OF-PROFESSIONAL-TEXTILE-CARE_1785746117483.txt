SPECIAL FEATURE
CARE LABEL INTEGRITY
Towards Authentic, Scientifically Validated Textile Care Labels
A care label is a compact technical instruction with important consequences for the garment owner, textile care professional, retailer, manufacturer, and environment.
When guidance is incomplete, inconsistent, or not aligned with the actual textile system, the risk of damage, dissatisfaction, and dispute increases.
A GARMENT IS A COMPLETE SYSTEM
Responsible professional assessment should consider:
•	Face fabric
•	Fibres and dyes
•	Trims and accessories
•	Interlinings and adhesives
•	Embellishments and decorative components
•	Garment construction
•	Previous use and treatment
•	Type and extent of soiling
•	Possible risks associated with the recommended care process
A cleaning method that is safe for one component may not be safe for the complete assembled garment.
QUESTIONS THIS JOURNAL WILL EXPLORE
•	Who has the authority and accountability for the final care instruction?
•	What testing supports the stated cleaning method and restrictions?
•	Does the care label reflect the complete assembled garment?
•	How should professionals respond when their observations conflict with the care label?
•	How can brands, manufacturers, laboratories, retailers, and textile care professionals share evidence more effectively?
EDITORIAL POSITION
Laundry Master supports clear, testable, traceable, and scientifically validated care guidance.
This Founding Edition opens the discussion. It does not claim that one organisation, profession, or viewpoint can resolve the subject alone.
We invite manufacturers, brands, testing laboratories, standards specialists, retailers, textile care professionals, and consumers to contribute evidence, case studies, professional experience, and constructive proposals.
THE COMPLETE PROFESSIONAL TEXTILE CARE JOURNEY
FROM RECEIVING TO CUSTOMER CONFIDENCE
Quality is not created by one machine, one chemical, or one department. It is the result of connected professional decisions from the first customer conversation to the final handover.
1.	RECEIVE AND LISTEN
Record the customer’s expectations, the garment’s condition and value, and any concerns expressed at the time of receiving.
2.	INSPECT AND CLASSIFY
Identify the garment’s construction, fibre composition, colour, soil, stains, risks, accessories, and available care information.
3.	TEST AND SPOT
Use controlled testing and progressive stain-removal procedures before beginning the main cleaning process.
4.	SELECT THE APPROPRIATE PROCESS
Choose dry cleaning, professional wet cleaning, laundry, hand cleaning, or another suitable process according to the garment’s complete construction and condition.
5.	CLEAN AND CONTROL
Manage chemistry, mechanical action, time, temperature, water quality, solvent condition, and other relevant process variables.
6.	FINISH AND RESTORE
Dry, press, steam, form, and detail the garment without introducing shine, distortion, shrinkage, colour change, or other damage.
7.	INSPECT AND PROTECT
Confirm the finished quality, package the garment correctly, and document the final result where necessary.
8.	RETURN WITH CONFIDENCE
Explain the outcome honestly, provide appropriate advice, and learn from the customer’s feedback.
Every stage contributes to customer confidence. A failure at any one stage can affect the quality of the entire service.
